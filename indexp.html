02

Working notes

The principles behind how I approach content, from audience research to experimentation and measurement.

  • Start with the audience.

    Understand who they are, what they need, and what already exists before deciding what to say.

  • Make strategy visible.

    Content pillars, formats, and distribution should be deliberate. Make the decisions clear enough to explain, test, and improve.

  • Treat every post as a test.

    Publish with intent, measure what happened, and let the data shape the next decision.

  • Ship, then refine.

    Good strategy gets sharper through execution. Publish, learn, adjust, repeat.

Case study · Product preview

A journal for noticing patterns.

Narehat is a personal skin journal built around one question: what if the useful signal is already there, just not visible yet?

Role
Product strategy, positioning, content
Timeline
Ongoing since 2026
Stage
Product preview
Focus
Habit loops, daily entries, pattern visibility
01

The problem

Most acne tracking tools record data. Very few of them help people go back and read it. The result is a growing pile of entries that never turns into a decision. But decisions are the only thing that actually changes skin.

02

The approach

I designed the loop around one honest entry a day, then built the reflection layer on top of that. Three principles shaped it:

  • Low input, high signal. One screen. A photo, a mood, a couple of tags. Nothing that feels like homework.
  • Surface patterns, not charts. Instead of a dashboard of numbers, show likely connections between habits and flare-ups in plain language.
  • Reward consistency quietly. Streaks, without guilt. The goal is showing up, not scoring points.
The hard part isn't tracking. It's making the pattern visible enough that someone changes something.
03

What I'm learning

Building Narehat has been less about software and more about language. How do you tell someone that their sleep and their breakouts seem connected, without sounding like an app that thinks it knows better than their dermatologist?

I don't have a clean answer yet. But writing the copy for this product is teaching me more about content strategy than most of the courses I've read about it.

Case study · Content strategy · Organic social

Short-form as a research tool.

PedeTanpaJerawat is where I test hooks, formats, and educational angles in public, one post at a time, with the analytics open next to the script.

Role
Content strategy, scripting, publishing
Timeline
Ongoing
Platforms
Short-form video (vertical)
Focus
Hooks, retention, education
01

The premise

Skincare content is saturated with opinion. What's missing is calm, source-aware explanation, the kind of thing you'd want a friend to send you at 11pm when you're spiraling about your skin.

02

What I'm testing

Each batch of posts is a small experiment. I write the hook, shoot the video, then check what actually held attention:

  • First three seconds. Does the promise land before the scroll reflex kicks in?
  • Format. Talking head vs. screen recording vs. text-on-image.
  • Angle. Myth-busting, personal story, or plain how-to.
03

Signals so far

A handful of posts crossed six figures of views. Those weren't the ones I expected. Which is exactly why I keep the experiment running.

The algorithm doesn't reward the best idea. It rewards the clearest first three seconds.
04

What I'm learning

Numbers are a starting point, not a verdict. A high-view post that doesn't move anyone toward action is worth less than a modest one that sends a handful of people to a comment section with a real question.

The metrics I care about now: completion rate, saves, and replies. Views are a symptom; those three are closer to the actual signal.
